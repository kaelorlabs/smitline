# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Phone calls wait on hold, with the voice switched off.** GPT-Live bills every second its session is open, silence included, so a 40-minute hold cost 40 minutes of a voice nobody heard ($2 at list price), and the assistant's "are you still there?" check-ins could end the call. Now, when a call is put on hold or into a queue, Smitline closes the GPT-Live session, keeps the phone line open, and listens itself with a small local speech detector, [FireRedVAD](https://github.com/FireRedTeam/FireRedVAD) (Apache-2.0) by default or [Silero VAD](https://github.com/snakers4/silero-vad) (MIT) with `SMITLINE_HOLD_DETECTOR=silero`. Hold music, silence, a recorded announcement already heard on the call, and songs do not wake it. Smitline recognizes the hold itself: each time the other side pauses, OpenAI's Decisions API (`gpt-6-luna`, `SMITLINE_HOLD_JUDGE_MODEL`; about $0.00002 a question, input only) says whether a person, a hold queue, a menu or something else is talking, in any language, and a sure answer of a queue's announcement or a person asking to hold starts it, as do 10 seconds of hold music; the voice's backend can also call `wait_on_hold`, but in simulated calls GPT-Live never did. When someone starts talking, a new session starts with the call so far and the audio from just before they spoke, so the voice hears their first words, and the same question tells someone picking up from the loop's next recording, which sends the voice back to waiting and never wakes it again. Time on hold does not count toward `maxMinutes`; a new brief field, `maxHoldMinutes` (phone only, default 60, at most 180), ends a hold nobody answers, with `endReason: hold_timeout`. The call shows its `hold` (`onHold`, `since`, `seconds`, `times`, `detector`), `usage.holdSeconds` has the time on hold, and the transcript has notes ("On hold.", "Someone picked up after about 12 minutes on hold."), which the summary model is told nobody said. `wait_for_call` returns at once when a call goes on hold or comes off it, and its `next` says to wait with `timeoutSeconds` 280 meanwhile. Without the detector's packages or model files the voice stays on and waits quietly. Not over direct SIP. The daemon now needs `numpy`, `onnxruntime` and `kaldi-native-fbank`, and comes with both detectors' model files. See [Waiting on hold](docs/phone.md#waiting-on-hold).
- **Phone menus are on for every call.** Any business may answer with an automated menu, so the assistant presses menu keys (and keys in numbers the brief gives for this call) without being told; `phoneMenus: false` (CLI `--no-phone-menus`) turns it off. The first person who comes on after a menu hears the opening that the menu did not; the standing instructions still never tell the voice to greet whoever picks up.
- **Taking over rings your phone first.** `transfer_call_to_me` (`POST /v1/calls/{id}/transfer`) used to redirect the call to `SMITLINE_OWNER_PHONE` blind: if you missed it, the other person heard a goodbye and the call was over. Now your phone rings from the call's number and says who has a person on the line and why, while the voice keeps them company; press 1 and you are talking with them through Smitline, with the voice switched off. When you hang up and they are still there, the voice comes back to see them off. If you do not answer, press another key, or the call to you fails, the voice says you can't come to the phone and carries on. The response is `{"transferred": false, "ringing": true}`, followed by `call.owner_connected` or `call.owner_unavailable`; `usage` has `ownerLegSeconds` (in the cost as a second call) and `ownerTalkedSeconds`. A new brief field, `connectOwner` (phone only, CLI `--connect-owner`), does the same on its own once a person picks up after a hold. Direct SIP calls are still handed over blind. See [Taking over](docs/phone.md#how-a-call-runs).

## 0.3.12 — 2026-11-10

- **Background noise no longer cuts the voice off on phone calls.** Since 0.1.0 Smitline stopped the voice itself whenever the other person's line got loud: 160 ms of sound paused it and 700 ms dropped the rest of what it was saying. It measured loudness only, so a TV, traffic or people talking near the phone stopped it again and again; recorded calls show up to 44 pauses and 20 dropped replies in one call. GPT-Live tells those apart from someone taking the floor and talks on through them. Now only the model decides: the unheard rest of a reply is dropped once the other person started talking while it was still making speech, it has since made no speech for 0.5 seconds, and it has transcribed words from them. A real interruption now stops the voice about half a second to a second after they start, instead of at once. The local detector still times the opening and the hang-up and tells a quiet line. `usage.audio` drops `pauses`, `backchannels` and `stopDelayMs` and adds `talkedOver`; `interruptionsFollowed` counts the times it stopped for them. See [Phone calls](docs/phone.md).

## 0.3.11 — 2026-11-07

- **Agents can tell the user about Smitline itself.** A new read-only tool, `how_to_smitline` (`GET /v1/how-to`, the SDKs' `howTo` and `how_to`), answers by `topic`: `overview` (what Smitline is, who makes it and first things to try, the default), `using`, `following` (the two ways to run a call: letting it run, or following it live), `pricing`, `account`, `troubleshooting` and `rules`, as a few short paragraphs and named links. In a live test, an agent refused by a call limit could only say the user was over it, not how the limit works or what comes next. A self-hosted installation says its owner pays their own providers (OpenAI, and SignalWire or Twilio) and quotes no prices, and its `account` topic reads its own calling hours, repeat limits and calling codes. The text informs the user and gives the agent no orders. A new optional call hook, `how_to(owner, topic, guide, client=None)`, lets a hosted service replace or extend a topic for an owner, live: its plans, and where the account stands; its gateway may name the app asking (`client`, such as `chatgpt` or `claude`), which only the hook reads. The MCP server's instructions and the call-with-smitline skill point to the tool. The overview ends with first things to try, both at once: a quick call to your own phone ("call me now"), or a real errand such as calling a local restaurant to book a table or order dinner, and where to follow the call and listen in. The instructions tell agents to offer them when the user has just connected Smitline or asks whether it is connected. See [Other endpoints](docs/calls.md#other-endpoints) and [Hooks](docs/calls.md#hooks).
- **The in-call tools say what they do.** `send_call_instruction` and `reply_to_call_request` are now write tools that only add, and `end_call`, `transfer_call_to_me` and `save_call_note` (which replaces a call's earlier note) are destructive. They used to say read-only so that apps would run them without asking. But apps and directory reviews (Claude's, ChatGPT's) read these hints as what a tool does, and Claude Code repeats a read-only call after a dropped connection, so an instruction could be spoken twice. Apps that ask before each tool call now ask for these too, until the user picks "Always allow". The tools, the MCP server's instructions, `how_to_smitline` and the call-with-smitline skill suggest that once, for `send_call_instruction` and `reply_to_call_request` as well as `wait_for_call`, `get_call` and `list_calls`, and never for `start_call`.
- **Phone calls say they are an AI in their opening.** A call now opens "Hey Sam, I'm calling on behalf of NAME about Friday's booking. I'm NAME's AI assistant.", in one turn, before the other person speaks. Who and why still come first, so the call sounds genuine; the AI part no longer waits for a natural moment later in the call. A person who comes on after a phone menu or a call screener hears the same. A disclosure that was missed is still said before the goodbye.
- **The brief's `context` asks for the facts the call needs,** not everything the agent and the user have been working on.
- **Every tool's annotations carry its title** (`annotations.title`, the same as `title`), as Claude's directory checks.
- **Examples name "Smith".** The tool descriptions, the call-with-smitline skill, the docs, code comments and the tests use "Smith" (or "Smith Lee") for the example person, instead of a team member's name.

## 0.3.10 — 2026-11-05

- **Agents keep following a live call.** In a live test an agent answered one question on a phone call, sent the same answer as an instruction too (the voice said it twice), checked the call twice more and stopped, so the next question went unanswered. The tools, the MCP server's instructions and the call-with-smitline skill now tell agents to keep calling `wait_for_call` in the same turn until the call ends, since nothing from the call reaches them once their turn ends; to set `canDo` whenever they could help the people on the call, at least to answer questions about the user's work (without it nothing they ask reaches the agent, though the old text said questions did); to answer requests with `reply_to_call_request` alone; and, where their app asks before each tool call, to suggest once that the user set `wait_for_call`, `get_call` and `list_calls` to always allow, never `start_call`. Every `start_call` and `wait_for_call` result starts with `next`, what to do now. The MCP `wait_for_call` always follows live (`since` defaults to `""`), so a wait counts as following and a request wakes it; REST, the SDKs and the CLI keep `since` optional. A live wait comes back at once for a request, a status change (`ringing`, `waiting`, `in_progress`) or the end, and otherwise about 12 seconds after new talk instead of 2: about five tool calls a minute instead of twenty. A wait from the start (`since: ""`) gathers what was already there for those 12 seconds too, so an agent that always asks from the start is not answered at once every time.
- **A meeting the platform keeps out says why, and what to do.** When Google Meet, Zoom or Teams does not let Smitline's browser in and it does not join by phone instead, the result is `not_reached` with a plain `reason` ("Google Meet did not let Smitline in as a guest.") and a `hint` for the agent: ask the user for the invitation's phone details (for Meet, its Join by phone number and PIN) and start again with them as `dialIn`; or, when a `dialIn` could not be used, why not (calling hours or call limits, in their own words, or an installation without phone calls). The meeting reports how the platform refused, as a word (`sign_in_required`, `restricted` or `guest_refused`: the handoff's new `joinRefused` and the call's `call.join_refused` event), never the page's text. Before, the agent read the objective and "Meeting ended (authentication_required) with 0 transcript entries (partial)". `check_call_brief` now also returns `warnings`, which never stop a call: a Google Meet link without `dialIn` gets one. The `to` and `dialIn` descriptions and the call-with-smitline skill ask for Meet's dial-in up front, so Smitline can join by phone by itself.
- **Meeting outcomes are about Smitline's part.** Being in the meeting and doing what the objective asked is `achieved` (some of it, `partial`); being asked to leave at the end is normal; `not_reached` means it never got in, or never got to do what it came for. Both Zoom meetings in the 2026-11-05 test came back `not_reached` though Smitline joined, introduced itself and answered questions, because people asked it to leave at the end. The summarizer is now also told what the transcript cannot show: who the meeting showed besides Smitline (from its participant list, which the meeting archive now keeps, also as the handoff's new `participants`), that Smitline set up the room itself (`to: "new"`), that it joined by phone, and why it left. A room's result had said nobody joined and that a room still had to be made, though two people had joined the room Smitline made.
- **Default voices for phone calls and for meetings, which the agent can change.** `SMITLINE_MEETING_VOICE` is the voice meetings speak with when the brief names none, joined by link or by phone; without it a meeting keeps its own default, a presence pack's character voice in the browser, else `SMITLINE_VOICE`, which stays the phone calls' default. `update_profile` (`PATCH /v1/profile`) takes `voices: {"phone", "meeting"}`, each a voice from `list_voices` or `""` for the default, saved through a new optional call hook, `save_voices(owner, voices)`, which writes `.env` for the local owner. `get_profile` (`voices`) and `list_voices` (`defaults`; `default` stays the phone calls' one) show the defaults. The tools say that a call keeps its voice until it ends, so a different voice someone on a call asks for applies from the next call, once the user agrees. `smitline setup voice --meeting <name>` sets it from the command line. A meeting's voice is checked against the owner's voices, so `SMITLINE_EXTRA_VOICES` in `.env` now works for meetings too. See [Brief](docs/calls.md#brief).
- **A meeting agent's tile goes as soon as it leaves.** In a Zoom test on 2026-11-05 the tile stayed for about four minutes after the agent left, blank, then black. Leaving came last, after the voice session closed; it clicked Leave only when Zoom's hidden toolbar happened to show, never answered Zoom's "Leave Meeting" question, and then the browser was killed, which Zoom treats as a dropped connection. Now every way out leaves the meeting first: the voice stops, Smitline clicks Leave and confirms it (in Zoom it brings the toolbar back first; it never ends the meeting for everyone) and checks that the page shows it is out. Only then is the meeting marked ended, the voice session closed and the archive written. A Daily room is left before its camera and microphone stop, and Teams' leave no longer gives up when it finds two Leave buttons. A leave the platform does not confirm within 8 seconds is recorded as `leave_failed` with the reason, and the meeting page is unloaded before the browser closes. `/health` has `leaveState` (`leaving`, `left`, `leave_failed`); the daemon waits for it, at most 15 seconds, before it writes the handoff and stops the container or deletes the machine.
- **A goodbye in any language leaves as quickly as an English one.** Once someone has asked the agent to leave ("you can hang up"), its answer counts as its goodbye in whatever language it speaks: when it has been heard and the meeting is quiet for a moment, the leave check reads it, and the agent leaves without waiting for its backend. In the same test a goodbye in Hindi waited 27 seconds. English goodbyes still leave without the check.
- **The mascot talks with the voice, and in 1280x720.** The talking picture now follows the voice as the meeting hears it: it starts with the first audible 10 ms written to the meeting and ends 0.2 seconds after the last, where it used to run on until 0.8 seconds after the last audio GPT-Live delivered (it delivers faster than it plays, then streams silence), so the mouth kept moving after the voice stopped. A version 2 presence pack can give its speaking clip a mouth: pictures of the open mouth from quiet to loud, drawn into the face with the loudness of the voice at that moment, in a browser meeting and in a Daily room. The blend into and out of speaking takes 0.08 seconds instead of 0.25, and the speaking clip is decoded before the first word. For 1280x720 packs, a picture that repeats is stored and sent once, a browser decodes only the frames about to play, a pack may hold 24 MB of pictures instead of 16, and a Daily room's camera gets 2.5 Mbit/s. The camera tracks tell the encoder they carry detail, so a short network keeps the picture sharp. The camera turns off once the meeting has ended and the goodbye has played, so nobody is left looking at a frozen frame, and a character that never walked in no longer walks out. Version 1 packs play as before. `scripts/camera-cpu.sh` measures a pack's CPU in a browser and in a Daily room. See [Appearing in the meeting](docs/meetings.md#appearing-in-the-meeting).
- **Daily rooms hear people.** Smitline joined Daily rooms deaf. It read daily-python's speaker without a completion callback, and that read always returns nothing: the audio goes only to the callback. So GPT-Live got silence for the whole meeting; on 2026-11-05 a room heard nobody while Daily showed two people talking. Now a 20 ms read always waits for its callback, and gaps are still filled with silence, so the stream never stops. A room that runs ahead of the clock skips silence to catch up; audio is dropped only more than half a second ahead, and counted. `meeting-runtime/daily_hearing_check.py` checks a room live with only its link: a talker plays bursts of a tone, and it reports what the bot heard and how late. See [Check that Smitline hears a room](docs/meetings.md#check-that-smitline-hears-a-room).
- **A meeting that hears nothing shows it in its archive.** `input_audio`, and `input` in `process_stopped`, say how much audio came in, how many milliseconds of it were voiced, the peak sample and, in a Daily room, how much was dropped. `input_silent` is recorded once when the meeting shows someone speaking and nothing comes in for 10 seconds.
- **The meeting hello waits for a pause in the meeting's audio.** It waited for 2 seconds without new words in the transcript, but the words come a second or more after the audio, so it could start over someone still talking. Now nobody may be heard in the audio either.
- **A meeting voice stops when talked over.** GPT-Live stops when someone talks over it but sends no event for it, and its speech arrives faster than it plays, so in meetings the rest of a reply kept playing. Now, as on a phone call, once someone has spoken over it for half a second and the voice says nothing new for 0.35 seconds, the rest is dropped (an `interrupted` event says how much); a short "mhm" does not stop it. In Daily rooms and in browser meetings alike.

## 0.3.9 — 2026-11-05

- **Phone menus.** A brief with `phoneMenus: true` (phone only, off by default) lets the assistant get through an automated phone menu: it listens for the option that fits the call, presses its key, and keys in a number the menu asks for when the brief gives it for this call, such as an account or order number. It never keys in card numbers, PINs or passwords, and a key that accepts something follows `mayAgreeTo`. SignalWire takes each key on the call's media stream, direct SIP sends it through OpenAI, and Twilio, whose media stream cannot send keys, plays the tones as audio. A `call.keys_pressed` event records each run of keys, showing a longer entry only as its length (the brief, and the transcript if the assistant says the number aloud, still hold it). Without the field nothing changes: the assistant still ends a call politely at a menu it cannot get through. Holding is not handled yet. See [Phone menus](docs/phone.md#phone-menus).

## 0.3.8 — 2026-11-05

- **Agents see that Smitline can start a meeting.** These now say plainly that a meeting brief with `to: "new"` starts a room people join from a link, ready in seconds: `start_call`'s description, its `channel` and `to` fields, the MCP server's instructions and the call-with-smitline skill. Joining someone's meeting by its link can take a minute or two while a hosted service starts a machine for it; `get_call` says this where it explains `connecting`. ## 0.3.7 — 2026-11-05

- **Each meeting on a machine of its own, for hosted installations.** `SMITLINE_MEETING_LAUNCHER=package.module:create` names a launcher plugin, and the daemon runs each meeting on a machine the plugin makes for it, such as a cloud VM, instead of in a container on its own Docker host. The meeting starts in the background: its call is `connecting` while the machine boots (about 80 seconds on Google Cloud), `end_call` ends it at once, and a machine that never comes up fails the call. The machine gets the meeting's runtime state, environment and token by value, so no key is written on the daemon's side. Its meeting checks the token on every route, `/health` too, and hands its archive back at `GET /archive` before the handoff is written. A restarted daemon adopts the machines still running, with their tokens (kept in `.smitline/active-meetings/<meeting id>.token`). Without the setting nothing changes. See [Each meeting on a machine of its own](docs/meetings.md#each-meeting-on-a-machine-of-its-own).
- **Machines for some platforms, this computer for the others.** `SMITLINE_MEETING_LAUNCHER_PLATFORMS=zoom,teams,meet` gives only those meetings a machine of their own and keeps Smitline rooms in the local meeting pool, where they start in seconds; `SMITLINE_MAX_MEETINGS` and `SMITLINE_MAX_MEETINGS_PER_OWNER` count both kinds together, and a restart adopts both. A launcher's start package now carries the meeting's `maxMinutes`, so a cloud VM can be given a lifetime a little longer than its meeting.
- **Joining a meeting by phone.** A meeting brief can carry its dial-in (`dialIn`: the number, the meeting ID and Zoom's numeric phone passcode, or the invitation text they come in), and `to` can be the dial-in number itself, also as a one-tap number such as `+14388097799,,85951289482#,,,,*123456#`. Smitline reads Zoom, Teams and Google Meet invitations and dials the first number the installation may call. The phone provider keys in the meeting ID and passcode with its own DTMF (`<Play digits>`, Twilio's and SignalWire's alike), timed like the platform's one-tap number, before the voice comes on the line. The voice is the meeting voice, with the same participation policy as in the browser, and says out loud that it is an AI, in the hello at the first pause. A waiting room or lobby is `waiting` and not meeting time (10 minutes at most, then `not_admitted`); a refused ID or passcode ends the call (`dial_in_refused`). The call is a meeting's: `line.joinedBy` is `"phone"`, `usage.voiceSeconds` is the time in the meeting, and the phone line is in the cost. A meeting with a link and its dial-in joins by link and dials in when the platform turns the browser away or no meeting seat is free; `SMITLINE_MEETING_DIAL_IN_FIRST` joins chosen platforms by phone first. The dial-in number gets the phone guardrails, calling hours and call limits included, unless it is a meeting platform's published dial-in number: Zoom's US and Canada numbers ship in `meeting-runtime/dial_in_bridges.json` (from Zoom's own pages, with the date read), `SMITLINE_DIAL_IN_BRIDGES` extends or replaces the list, and Teams and Google Meet numbers keep the phone rules. A refusal says when it can dial in and that a Zoom number from the invitation has no such limits; `afterHours` works for meetings with `dialIn`, and dial-ins count toward the call limits. See [Joining by phone](docs/meetings.md#joining-by-phone).

## 0.3.6 — 2026-11-05

- **A meeting agent that says goodbye leaves.** Asked to leave, the voice says goodbye, and Smitline leaves once that has been heard, even when the voice never asks its backend to. It used to stay: GPT-Live streams silence between replies, and Smitline counted any audio still on its way to the meeting, silence included, as speech, so the goodbye never seemed to end. In a Zoom test on 2026-11-05 it said "Got it, leaving now. Bye.", stayed, and then said it had left. Only audible speech counts now.
- **A mascot camera.** `SMITLINE_MEETING_CAMERA_STYLE=mascot` with `SMITLINE_MEETING_CAMERA_PACK` plays a presence pack: a clip for each presence state, such as walking in once admitted, listening, thinking, speaking and leaving, each new clip blending in over a quarter of a second. A pack's `persona` says who the character is, and the voice is given it, so Smitline sounds like the character it appears as; its `voice` is the character's GPT-Live voice for meetings, unless the brief names one. See [Appearing in the meeting](docs/meetings.md#appearing-in-the-meeting).
- **Who is speaking.** Where the meeting shows who is speaking (Daily rooms, Teams and Google Meet), the voice is told who has the floor once they have held it for a moment, so it can answer people by name, and the meeting's transcript and captions name them.
- **Agents can start a meeting.** A meeting brief with `to: "new"` makes Smitline open a Daily room (with `SMITLINE_DAILY_API_KEY`), wait there for people, and return the room's link as the call's `to` and in a `call.room_created` event, so an agent can send people a link they open in any browser. Without the key the brief is refused before anything is made.
- **Daily rooms.** Smitline joins a Daily room (`https://TEAM.daily.co/ROOM`) like a Zoom, Teams, or Meet meeting, but without a browser: daily-python connects it, it receives microphones only, and the meeting container draws its camera tile (the still picture, or a presence pack's clips with `SMITLINE_MEETING_CAMERA_STYLE=mascot`). `meeting-runtime/daily_rooms.py create` makes a room that expires and prints its link, so an agent can start a meeting and send people a link they open in any browser, with no account. Smitline waits in the room for the first of them (`waiting_for_admission`, up to 10 minutes) before its voice starts. The meeting image adds `daily-python` and `pillow`.
- **A meeting that outlives a daemon restart keeps its call.** A daemon that stopped without ending its meetings (a crash, a forced restart) left their containers running; the next daemon adopted them but had already closed their calls, so the rest of each meeting ran with nothing counting its time or ending it at `maxMinutes`. Now the call is followed again (`call.resumed`): its meeting time counts from when it went live, and it still ends at `maxMinutes`. Instructions and listening in need the meeting's token, which only the daemon that started it had, so they stay unavailable for the rest of such a meeting.
- **The meeting hello says what Smitline is there for.** With `SMITLINE_MEETING_INTRO=1`: "Hey, this is NAME's AI assistant. I'm here to answer any questions or do any work. I'll be here taking notes, so let me know if you need anything." It offers to do work only when the brief's `canDo` lets people ask the agent for things, and it waits for a pause however long that takes, instead of speaking over people after 45 seconds.
- docs: `.env.example` and `AGENTS.md` said meetings introduce themselves by default; since 0.3.0 the spoken hello is opt-in. The capability matrix now shows Zoom's participant list and its empty-room leave.
- docs: Smithery no longer lists Smitline. It became part of Arcade and shut down its proxy and accounts on 2026-10-31. The README's Smithery badge now points to MCP Market, which lists Smitline, and [MCP directories](docs/mcp-directories.md) says where Smitline is listed today.

## 0.3.5 — 2026-11-04

- **MCP 2026-07-28.** The MCP server speaks the newest MCP revision as well as the older ones, on the local endpoint, over stdio and in the remote connector.
  - A 2026-07-28 client needs no `initialize` and no session. Every request names its version and the client's capabilities in `_meta`; on HTTP it also sends `MCP-Protocol-Version`, `Mcp-Method` and `Mcp-Name` headers, which the server checks against the body.
  - `server/discover` describes the server. Results carry `resultType` and the server's name, and lists and the call card carry caching hints. `subscriptions/listen` is acknowledged and then ends at once, because nothing changes while the server runs.
  - `initialize` still opens a 2025-11-25, 2025-06-18 or 2025-03-26 session, so existing clients are unaffected. A client that speaks both now uses 2026-07-28; the official TypeScript SDK 2.4 in auto mode does.
  - The Claude Desktop bundle passes the new headers on and cancels a request when the client does. Over stdio, a cancelled request gets no reply.
- **The Relay look.** The console and the README take Smitline's brand from smitline.com: the Relay arch beside the name (also the favicon, the README logo and the Claude Desktop bundle's icon), warm sand pages with denim buttons, links and focus, and night denim in dark mode. Headings and the name are in Fraunces, text in Jost and machine values in Geist Mono, served by the console itself from `control-panel/fonts/` (SIL Open Font License). The key page and the connector's approval page take the colours and the arch but keep the system's fonts, because they load nothing. Every text colour meets WCAG AA. The meeting camera picture is unchanged.
- The MCP Registry entry's website is now smitline.com, the home of Smitline Cloud (the hosted version), instead of this repository.

## 0.3.4 — 2026-11-02

- **Newer MCP clients connect without pinning an old version.** The MCP server now speaks protocol version 2025-11-25 as well as 2025-06-18 and 2025-03-26. `initialize` negotiates in its body whatever `MCP-Protocol-Version` header comes with it, so a client that sends its own newest version there gets the server's instead of `400`; every later request must still name a version the server speaks. On 2026-11-02 an agent app was refused on its first request and had to be pinned to 2025-06-18. A request in revision 2026-07-28 (stateless, no `initialize`) gets `400` with an ordinary JSON-RPC error, which tells a client that also speaks older revisions to fall back to `initialize`; full 2026-07-28 support comes later.

## 0.3.3 — 2026-11-02

- **What the agent tells the voice gets said, at the right moment.** On 2026-11-02 an agent following a call sent the voice the email address the person had just asked for. The voice's instructions said to follow notes "silently", so it said it did not know; it started to give the address a few seconds later, was cut off, and gave it only when asked again. Now the voice acts on notes without mentioning them, and tells the people on the call what a note gives it for them at the next pause, never over someone who is speaking, and again if it is cut off. While it checks something it says so, and it does not say it does not know before the answer comes back. Notes from the agent (`send_call_instruction`) reach it labelled "From NAME's agent:", so it can tell them from its system's own notes. Phone calls now cue an answer from the agent (`done`, an announced `status`, a retell) once nobody has been heard for 2.5 seconds, as meetings do (`call.answer_cued`); before, it waited until the person spoke again.
- **A call card in ChatGPT and Claude.** `start_call` and `get_call` now come with an MCP Apps card (`ui://smitline/call-card-v1.html`): the call's status while it runs, then how it went, the details, what it cost, the recording when there is a link to it, and the transcript. The card follows a live call by itself through `get_call`. It is one self-contained page that shows everything as text; clients without MCP Apps get the same results as text. The MCP server now answers `resources/list` and `resources/read`.
- **Smitline records calls itself.** A call with `record: true` (or `SMITLINE_RECORD_CALLS=1`) is recorded as its audio passes through Smitline: one WAV kept with the call, the other person on the left channel and the voice on the right, from pickup to hang-up. The phone provider is no longer asked to record: on 2026-11-02 SignalWire's recording of a 2 min 35 s call held 7 seconds, because it stops when its answering-machine check reports, and provider recordings sat behind the provider's own URLs. A call's `recording` field is now `seconds`, `format` and `source: "smitline"`, with no URL, and `GET /v1/calls/{id}/recording` serves the WAV (MP3 only for calls recorded before). The console shows and downloads both kinds.
- **Calls nobody answers end sooner.** The voice checks whether anyone is there after 12 seconds of quiet instead of 40. When it has spoken three times in a row without a word back and the line goes quiet again, the call ends (`call.no_answer_hangup`, with `turns`), and the voice is told to end a call when nobody answers after a couple of checks. On 2026-11-02 a restaurant picked up and then went quiet: the voice waited 40 seconds before speaking and then checked in three times over two minutes.
- **Questions reach the agent** ([#84](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/pull/84)). With `canDo` in the brief, a question the voice cannot answer now goes to the agent that set up the call, which knows the owner's work and decides what to share: the meeting and phone backends pass it on instead of saying the owner will follow up. `canDo` limits tasks only; a task outside it is still declined. Agents are told to answer only with what the user would want those people to know. On 2026-10-23 a question in a meeting was never passed on because `canDo` named only page edits and a phone call, while the voice said "on it" and "still waiting" ([#83](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/issues/83)).
- **The voice stops claiming sends it never made.** It is told never to say a request was passed on, is being worked on, or is done unless its backend's answer or a note says so. The safety net now also catches "on it" and "I'm sending it", so what the person asked is passed on (`source: "speech"`) when the voice skips its backend. "Still waiting", which a late request's note tells it to say, is not taken as a claim.
- **A meeting hears the agent's answer without asking for it.** An answer the people who asked must hear (`done`, an announced `status`, a retell) is cued once nobody, the voice included, has been heard for 2.5 seconds: at once in a quiet meeting, at a real gap while people talk. Not if the voice has already said it, and not after 120 seconds without a gap; then the note's own "first thing the next time you speak" stands. Before, answers sat unsaid until someone asked whether anything had come back.
- **Requests from every live call stay in sight** ([#82](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/pull/82)). A live wait with `others` also watches the owner's other calls in progress: a new request on any of them wakes it at once, and `live.otherRequests` lists each one the agent has not answered yet, until it sends at least a `status`. While such a wait runs, the agent counts as following those calls. The MCP `wait_for_call` turns it on whenever it follows live (`others: false` turns it off); REST (`others=true`), the SDKs (`others`) and the CLI (`--others`) leave it off unless asked. On 2026-10-22 an agent busy with a failing phone call missed two requests from the meeting that had asked for it.
- **A call reports to the request it serves.** A brief's optional `forRequest` (`{"callId", "requestId"}`) names an open request on another of the owner's calls, such as a meeting's request to phone someone. The call then tells that request by itself when it rings, when it connects, and how it ended or why it could not be made, never with the number called; these replies are marked `automatic: true`. The request must be the owner's own and still open (`422`, `409`; `check` reports the same). CLI: `--for-request <call id>:<request id>`.
- **Canceling a request ends the call serving it, and goodbyes are only for people.** A call placed with `forRequest` ends as soon as the people who asked cancel the request, without waiting for the agent (`call.request_canceled`). Ending a phone call this way or with `end_call` says goodbye only once a person has spoken: a voicemail greeting or a call-screening prompt ("reason for calling", "please stay on the line") is not a person (`call.person_heard` marks the first one), and ringing, screening or silence hang up at once with nothing said (`call.no_person_hangup`). On 2026-10-23 a 12-second goodbye to a call screener kept a phone ringing for about 20 seconds after the meeting asked to drop the call.
- **One conversation from hello to goodbye.** The voice's standing instructions no longer tell it to greet whoever picks up: the hello is only in the opening cue, sent once. On 2026-11-02 a call to the user's own phone started over twice when he said "hello?" after a quiet moment, because the call-screening and own-phone instructions said to greet the person and give the reason. The call-screening note and the network's machine guess now say what is happening and leave the rest to the model, which has the whole conversation.
- **Phone calls fail fast when the network blocks the tunnel** ([#81](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/pull/81)). Some networks (often venue, office or guest Wi-Fi) block outbound port 7844, which every Cloudflare quick tunnel needs. When cloudflared shows that as it starts, the call fails in seconds instead of after the 90-second start timeout, is not retried, and says why: "This network blocks Cloudflare tunnels (port 7844), so phone calls cannot connect. Switch networks (a phone hotspot works) or set SMITLINE_PUBLIC_URL. Meetings still work." A tunnel that still times out names cloudflared's last error, and cloudflared is stopped when a tunnel fails early. On 2026-10-22 this cost a demo its slot.
- **Check a phone call before a demo.** For a phone call in relay mode, `POST /v1/calls/check` (MCP `check_call_brief`) now also tests that the phone provider can reach this computer once the brief passes every other check: it starts or reuses the quick tunnel, reports a blocked network in `problems`, and leaves the tunnel warm for the call. Agents are told never to stop following a meeting to fix something else, and to tell the people who asked at once (`done`, `ok: false`) when what they asked for fails.
- **Google Meet says when Google turns the guest away** ([#87](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/pull/87)). Right after "Join now", Google can refuse a guest browser with "You can't join this video call", even in a meeting open to anyone. Smitline used to report that as needing sign-in; the meeting's error now says Google turned the guest join away (`GuestRefused`, still the `authentication_required` stage). As before, a connected Google account is tried next when there is one.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-10-21

- **Answers are given at a good moment and are not lost.** The voice gives an answer from the agent at the next natural pause when the person is waiting for it, otherwise first thing the next time it speaks, and finishes what it is saying first. If someone cuts in before it has said it, it says it the next time; if 35 seconds later nothing it said carried the answer, the answer is given to it once more (`call.request_retold`). Paraphrase counts ("the next one after that" for "77", "October 6th" for "October 6"), and a sentence still being said gets up to 10 more seconds.
- **Requests the voice only says it passed on are passed on.** GPT-Live sometimes tells people "I've passed that to your agent" without asking its backend. When no `ask_agent` call follows within 10 seconds, Smitline passes on what the person said, as a request with `source: "speech"`.
- **Calls to your own phone sound like your assistant.** A call to `SMITLINE_OWNER_PHONE` (not a rehearsal) greets you by first name and gets to the point, with no introduction and no AI disclosure.
- **The meeting voice knows who is in the meeting.** It reads the participant list (Zoom's participants panel is new; Teams and Google Meet already had one) when it joins and every 20 seconds, and gets the names as quiet context, with the one that is probably the owner marked.
- **Meetings join quietly, and the voice talks less.** No spoken disclosure when Smitline joins: its meeting name is now "Smitline AI" and its camera tile shows "Smith's AI assistant" under the mark, so everyone, also people who join late, can see it is an AI and whose. It introduces itself only when greeted or asked. `SMITLINE_MEETING_INTRO=1` brings back a short spoken hello ("Hi everyone, Smith's AI assistant here."), said at the first pause instead of over whoever is speaking. The voice no longer answers thanks, "okay" or "go ahead", switches to the language people ask for (Hindi, Tamil, Telugu and others) instead of declining, and says a request was passed on once rather than twice. Phone calls still say aloud that they are an AI.
- **Requests from the call** ([#74](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/issues/74)). With `canDo` in the brief, people on a phone call or in a meeting can ask the agent that started it for things the voice cannot do itself. The voice passes the request on (`ask_agent` on its backend), the agent's live `wait_for_call` returns at once with it in `live.requests`, and the agent answers with `reply_to_call_request`: `status` any number of times (quiet context for the voice, or `announce: true` to have it said), then `done` once, which the voice tells the person who asked at the next pause. Details go in a meeting's chat. The voice can add to a request or cancel it. `only: "requests"` follows a long meeting by its requests alone. If no agent follows the call the voice says so at once; a request not picked up in 30 seconds is flagged; requests still open at the end are the result's `openRequests`. CLI: `--can-do`, `smitline calls reply`, `calls wait --since --only requests`. SDKs: `replyToRequest` / `reply_to_request`.
- **Live guidance for meetings.** `send_call_instruction` now reaches a meeting in progress, through `POST /control` on the meeting container with a token made for that meeting. `/listen` needs the same token.
- **Listen in** ([#72](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/issues/72)). With `SMITLINE_LISTEN=1`, the console's **Listen in** button plays both sides of a call or meeting in the browser while it runs, and `GET /v1/calls/{id}/listen` streams them as NDJSON. For phone calls the audio is copied as it passes through the relay; a meeting container serves its own on `/listen`, which the daemon relays. A listener that falls behind loses audio, and the call never waits for it. Each listen adds a `call.listening` event. It is off by default because the people on the call are not told. Direct SIP calls cannot be heard, since their audio never passes through Smitline.
- **Follow a call live** ([#72](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/issues/72)). `wait_for_call` takes `since` (`""` at first, then the last answer's `live.cursor`). It then also returns about 2 seconds after something new is said, with the new lines in `live.transcript`, so the agent that placed a call can supervise it and step in with `send_call_instruction`. The REST API (`GET /v1/calls/{id}/wait?since=`) and both SDKs (`waitForCall(id, timeout, { since })`, `wait_for_call(id, timeout, since=...)`) do the same. Meetings are now followed live too: their transcript reaches the call as it is spoken (`call.transcript` events, and the SSE stream) instead of only when the meeting ends. The MCP server's instructions, `start_call`'s description and the `call-with-smitline` skill teach agents to supervise calls this way.
- **A goodbye ends the call once it has been heard.** A phone call hangs up 1.5 seconds after the agent's goodbye has played, if the other person says nothing more than thanks or bye; anything else carries the call on. A meeting is left the same way after the agent's goodbye: at once when someone asked it to leave ("Smitline, you can leave now", or just "hang up"), otherwise when a quick model check of the last 30 lines (`gpt-5.6-luna`) agrees that leaving is what a person would do now. Neither waits for the backend's end_call or leave_meeting, which the voice does not always ask for. Hanging up no longer waits for the phone line's whole buffer, which GPT-Live keeps filling with silence, but only for its speech: a goodbye used to leave several seconds of dead air. Ending a call at a voicemail (`POST /v1/calls/{id}/end`, the MCP `end_call` tool or `smitline calls end`) hangs up without a goodbye, instead of leaving a vague message.
- **The meeting agent leaves by itself, and the phone agent hangs up when a call goes nowhere** ([#65](https://github.com/kaelorlabs/smitline/issues/65)). When a participant asks it to drop off, or the meeting is clearly over or no longer needs it, the meeting agent says a short goodbye and leaves through a new `leave_meeting` backend tool. It waits for the goodbye to be heard (at most 10 seconds), then closes the GPT-Live session, which reports final usage, and leaves the meeting, so no voice, model or meeting time is billed after that. It also leaves once nobody has spoken for `SMITLINE_MEETING_IDLE_LEAVE_SECONDS` (default 600, `0` never does). The meeting call keeps `endReason: meeting_ended`, and a `call.agent_left` event says why: `left_completed`, `left_asked_to_leave`, `left_not_productive`, `left_other`, `idle` or `alone_in_meeting`. Phone calls are told to end politely, with the new `end_call` reason `not_productive`, when they go in circles, the two sides still cannot understand each other after a couple of tries, the other side is abusive, or an automated menu will not let them through.
- **Never alone in a meeting.** Zoom now reports its participant count, read from the participants button, so the agent leaves an empty Zoom meeting too. On every platform it leaves once each reading for 15 seconds has said it is alone, so one wrong reading does not end its meeting. The meeting agent decides by itself when else to leave, the way a person would: the work is done, someone asks it to go, however they put it, or the meeting is wrapping up.
- **Owners, so one deployment can serve many accounts.** Every call, meeting, profile, contact book and do-not-call list belongs to an owner, and another owner's are answered as not found. A self-hosted installation has one owner, `local`, with its files where they always were. API tokens can be created for an owner (`api_tokens.py create --owner OWNER`), or as `--kind internal` for a hosted gateway that names the owner of each request with `X-Smitline-Owner`; any other credential sending the header gets `403 owner_header_forbidden`. `SMITLINE_REQUIRE_OWNER=1` refuses requests that would act as `local` (`401 owner_required`), closes the raw `/v1/meetings` API, and turns away incoming calls to numbers no owner has. See [docs/calls.md#owners](docs/calls.md#owners).
- New optional call hooks: `settings(owner)` (the owner's name, phone, caller ID, voice, guardrails and recording setting), `storage_root(owner)` (other owners' files go in `.smitline/owners/<owner>/`), `contact_book(owner)`, `call_started(owner, record)` (runs before the line starts; a refusal ends the call cleanly), `webhook_secret(owner)` (each other owner signs its webhooks with its own secret) and `owner_for_number(number)` (who an incoming call is for). Repeat limits count only the owner's own calls.

- **Usage you can bill by.** A stop now asks each call in progress to end and gives it `SMITLINE_SHUTDOWN_GRACE` seconds (default 5) to finish and report what it used, instead of cutting it off at once. Incoming phone calls report `phoneSeconds` too: the provider sends no connected time for them, so it is measured from when the call's audio connected to when it ended (likewise for an outbound call whose status callback never arrives). A meeting's `voiceSeconds` stops when the meeting ends, not when its handoff arrives up to two minutes later.

- A meeting call is refused when it is placed if no meeting container can take it (`409 capacity_exceeded` when the container is busy or the pool full, `409 owner_capacity_exceeded`, `409 meeting_pool_required`, `503 no_free_ports`), instead of being accepted and failing a moment later.
- Phone callbacks are checked with the credentials of the call's owner, and incoming calls with those of the owner of the number called, whose settings build the call's brief.
- A meeting for an owner other than `local` runs with that owner's OpenAI key, voice and name; a setting the owner has not set is never the installation's.
- **Several meetings at once.** `SMITLINE_MAX_MEETINGS` (default 1, which keeps one meeting container at the usual ports 6082 and 8094) lets the daemon run that many meetings side by side. Each runs in its own compose project, `smitline-meeting-<meeting id>`, published on two ports from `SMITLINE_MEETING_PORTS` (default `18100-18399`; noVNC, then `/health`), and mounts only its own run and recording folders and its owner's browser profiles: the local owner's signed-in profiles, or for any other owner a folder of its own, so it joins as a guest and never sees another account's sign-in or the data volume's settings. Its environment is only its OpenAI key and the meeting settings it reads (voice, names, `SMITLINE_MEETING_*`), written to a private file for that meeting, never the whole `.env`: no provider key, SIP or webhook secret reaches it. A full pool is still `409 capacity_exceeded`; `SMITLINE_MAX_MEETINGS_PER_OWNER` caps each owner other than `local` (`409 owner_capacity_exceeded`). Each running meeting's claim is `.smitline/active-meetings/<meeting id>.json`. At startup the daemon follows every pool meeting still running and removes its other pool containers, never another installation's on the same Docker host. `SMITLINE_REQUIRE_OWNER=1` always uses the pool. The published image needs Docker Engine 26 or later for it (volume subpaths).
- The owner's settings reach a meeting exactly as written: values with quotes, dollar signs or backslashes (an owner name such as O'Neil) are passed in compose's double-quoted form instead of being blanked, and another owner's meeting joins under that owner's `SMITLINE_PARTICIPANT_NAME` (Meet and Teams know the bot by that name, so they still skip its own tile when they find who is speaking). The local owner's meetings still join as "Smitline".
- One meeting container at a time (the default) runs only the local owner's meetings, because it uses the installation's signed-in browser profiles and sees the whole data volume: `POST /v1/calls` and `/v1/calls/check` refuse another owner's meeting with `409 meeting_pool_required` ("set `SMITLINE_MAX_MEETINGS` to 2 or more"). A deployment with `SMITLINE_REQUIRE_OWNER=1` always uses the pool.
- The TypeScript SDK talks to a remote daemon: `new Smitline({ baseUrl, token, headers })` sends the fixed token and the extra headers on every request, and never starts a daemon or reads `daemon.auth`.

## 0.1.1 — 2026-10-01

### Call guardrails

- Emergency and crisis numbers (911, 112, 999, 988, and others) are never dialed.
- Premium-rate and satellite numbers are refused unless `COLLEAGUE_ALLOW_PREMIUM_NUMBERS=1`.
- Calls ring only between 8 AM and 9 PM in the recipient's time zone (`COLLEAGUE_CALLING_HOURS`); a brief's `afterHours: true` overrides this when the user confirms.
- When someone asks not to be called again, the assistant ends the call politely, the result has `doNotCall: true`, and the number goes on a do-not-call list (`/v1/do-not-call`, `smitline do-not-call`).
- At most 5 calls to one number per 24 hours and 20 calls an hour (`COLLEAGUE_MAX_CALLS_PER_NUMBER`, `COLLEAGUE_MAX_CALLS_PER_HOUR`).

### Fixes

- `smitline` CLI output larger than 64 KB is no longer cut off when piped (it waits for output to drain before exiting).
- The console shows the Smitline mark as the participant avatar and the tab icon.

### Recording on request

- A brief with `record: true` (CLI `--record`) records that one call; `COLLEAGUE_RECORD_CALLS=1` still records every call.
- Download a recording (WAV keeps both sides on separate channels; MP3 mixes them) with `smitline calls recording --call-id <id> --out call.wav`, from the call's page in the console, or from `GET /v1/calls/{id}/recording`.

## 0.1.0 — 2026-09-30

First public release.

Smitline (previously developed as Colleague AI) gives any AI agent phone calls and meetings. The agent sends a brief; Smitline talks with people in real time using OpenAI GPT-Live (`gpt-live-1`), hands harder questions to a backend model that knows the brief, and returns a structured result: the outcome, a summary, details, decisions, action items, open questions, and the transcript.

### What it does

- **Phone calls** through your SignalWire or Twilio account. Every call opens with an AI disclosure naming the person it acts for. Rehearse a call on your own phone first, follow the live transcript, or take the call over on your phone.
- **Zoom, Microsoft Teams, and Google Meet meetings** with the same brief on the `meeting` channel. A browser participant joins in a second container, speaks through a virtual microphone after a short disclosure, and can show a presence camera.
- **One Docker image**, `ghcr.io/kaelorlabs/smitline`, started with one `docker run`. It runs the loopback daemon, the local console, the CLI (`docker exec smitline smitline ...`), and starts the meeting image, `ghcr.io/kaelorlabs/smitline-meeting`, for each meeting.
- **Any agent.** MCP over Streamable HTTP at `127.0.0.1:8095/mcp` with a local token, or over stdio (`smitline mcp`); the CLI; the REST calls API (`/v1/calls`, with an OpenAPI description); TypeScript and Python SDKs; and a remote connector with OAuth sign-in for cloud agents.
- **Agent-driven setup.** Paste one prompt into your agent. Keys are typed into a one-time local page, never into the chat, and stay in the data volume.
- **Context in three levels:** the owner's profile (who they are, the people they know, standing boundaries), the session context in the brief (a summary, facts, decisions, open questions, and long reference details), and the goal (objective, questions, tone, contact).
- **Calls dashboard** at `127.0.0.1:8095/calls`: each call's brief, live transcript, result, and phone plus OpenAI cost, with spend totals.

### Phone audio

- **Local barge-in.** Smitline listens to the other person itself: about 160 ms after they talk over it, playback pauses and the provider's buffer is cleared. A short "mhm" lets it carry on; real speech drops the rest of what it was saying.
- **Provider-clock pacing and adaptive playout.** Playback follows the phone provider's clock, recovered from the timestamps on its audio, so a computer clock that runs fast or slow does not starve or flood the line. The provider's buffer is kept near 0.3 seconds by stretching or trimming pauses; speech itself is never cut.
- **A natural opening.** The assistant says a short hello naming whose AI assistant it is, then waits for an answer before saying why it is calling. Call screeners and voicemail greetings are heard out first.

### Known limitations

See [Limitations](README.md#limitations). In short: phone audio is relayed through your computer (direct SIP is being built), Teams and Google Meet still need live acceptance testing, trial phone accounts call only verified numbers, and Twilio's free trial cannot carry call audio.
